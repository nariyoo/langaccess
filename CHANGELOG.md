# Changelog

## [0.2.0] - 2026-09-18

The readings move first. Eleven more languages are named on evidence of the reader's own, so every
one of the 21 languages on a reviewer's list of organizations this instrument had published as
`english_only` is now read both from a paragraph and from a help notice, and the Lithuanian list is
wide enough for a notice of 232 characters; codebook rule 9 no longer clears inside a script on a
coordinating conjunction alone; an embedded social feed leaves the page before the page is read, so
somebody else's post is not read as the organization's second language; a block written the way an
address and a telephone number are written is not offered to the bundled identifier at all; and a
switcher that is a vendor's full menu is driven in a preference order of the languages the United
States is served in, where page order had been settling the row on whichever language the widget
listed first. What an address may be, and what the package can say about one it could not read,
moves next. `auditable_url` refuses `mailto:` and every other scheme that is not the web, and
refuses an address that carries credentials, where seven non-web schemes tried by hand had been
seven accepted; `no_dns` separates a domain that is not there from a site the crawl could not read;
`parked_domain` and the nine sale and parking hosts of `PARKED_HOST` name the registrar page that
had been falling into `other`; and a right-hand word boundary on eleven wall alternatives stops
`security check` from matching inside `security checklist` and three alternatives ending on `found`
from deciding inside `founded`. What a run says about itself moves too. `--strict` exits 9 where a
run that finished degraded had exited 0; `tool_build` and `judged_build` carry the first twelve hex
digits of the sha256 of the `core.py` that read the site and of the one that applied the rules, so a
row no longer rests on a label that named two instruments; a challenge that clears mid-poll is read
as the page it became, and a page in a borrowed browser is brought to the front before the poll; and
the two settle waits are named constants inside the freeze. The live audit and the re-judge are one
reader: the text both judge is `_page_text` over one document, so what a page ships and does not lay
out, a hidden span beside its English twin, a carousel slide that is not the visible one, a wizard
step, is read live as it always was on a re-judge, where the two readers had differed on 24 of the
1,992 gold-frame verdicts and the published figures described only the second; a quoted testimonial
inside a container that says so leaves the count and goes on the record at rung 1; a site builder's
own empty state is not the organization writing; thirteen Latin-script function-word lists are
widened under a hundred-to-one folded-frequency screen, three French words that read English pages
as French are gone, and Ilocano and Cebuano are named beside Tagalog; and the hand-coding queue
gains a seventh kind, for the absence claim nobody was sent to check. Two repairs to the
instrument's reach were built, measured and refused: a second browser engine read none of the 30
unreachable addresses of the gold frame it was offered, and the other entry pages recovered none of
the 20 of those whose host resolves. What the crawler says about itself, and about a read that
failed, moves last. The User-Agent no longer names this project: the contact comment it had held
since the release cost 13 of the 144 addresses a gold-frame run recorded unreachable, 7 refusing it
with a protocol error, 4 with a 403 and 2 with a destroyed execution context, against none that
answered the comment and not a plain browser string. A failed read names its cause, so `Error (home
read retried once)` on 24 of those 144 now reads `Error: net::ERR_HTTP2_PROTOCOL_ERROR (home read
retried once)`, and `protocol_error` and `connection_closed` take the failure vocabulary to sixteen
names. And a shared-browser batch reads its own transport failures once more at the end of itself,
on a browser launched for them alone at concurrency 2. Read again with all three in place, 94 of
those 144 addresses now rest on at least one page, which LIMITATIONS 4 states with its breakdown and
with what the measurement cannot apportion. The agreement figures for this build against the gold
frame are in LIMITATIONS 1 and were measured on a live re-read of that frame, while the 93.2%
agreement and kappa 0.896 taken on the frozen capture stay the figures of record for 0.1.0.

On the live re-read of the 1,992 gold-frame addresses, the bytes that took the capture and 0.2.0,
judged from the same stored pages, agree with the settled classes at 91.2% and 91.5% (three classes,
n = 1,807), and of the 19 sites whose class differs between the two judgements, 12 move toward the
settled class, 6 away from it and 1 between two classes neither of which is the settled one; 18 more
change only the languages named. LIMITATIONS 1.1 has the table.

### Added

- **`protocol_error` and `connection_closed`, two failure kinds, and the note that can say them.**
  A read that failed wrote the exception's CLASS and nothing else, and Playwright reports every
  network condition it meets as one class, so the word `Error` was the whole note. Of the 144
  addresses a run over the gold frame recorded `unreachable` on 2026-09-08, **24 say `Error (home
  read retried once)` and not one of them says what the error was**. Probed address by address on
  2026-09-18, those same addresses answer with `net::ERR_HTTP2_PROTOCOL_ERROR` on 7, a destroyed
  execution context on 2, and one each of `net::ERR_CONNECTION_CLOSED`, `net::ERR_NAME_NOT_RESOLVED`
  and `net::ERR_CONNECTION_TIMED_OUT`. Those are different failures with different answers, and
  `failure_kind` could not tell them apart because the note did not hold them.

  `_error_note` writes `Error: net::ERR_HTTP2_PROTOCOL_ERROR` where the row read `Error`. The
  message is trimmed and not pasted. The API call Playwright puts in front of it, `Page.goto: `,
  says where the read was rather than what went wrong and is identical for every cause; the
  ` at <address>` a network code is reported with is the address the row already names in `url`.
  What is left is bounded to `ERROR_NOTE_CHARS`, eighty characters, because a note is read in a
  terminal and in a table cell and an exception message has held a page's own JavaScript before now.
  An exception with nothing to say still answers with its class alone, which is the string this
  wrote for everything.

  Two names follow and the failure vocabulary goes from fourteen to sixteen. `protocol_error` reads
  `net::ERR_HTTP2_PROTOCOL_ERROR` and `net::ERR_EMPTY_RESPONSE`; `connection_closed` reads
  `net::ERR_CONNECTION_CLOSED` and the `Connection closed` a dying Playwright driver writes, which
  **10 of the 144 say**. Both mean that nothing was refused and nothing was served, so neither is
  `bot_wall`, where a wall was shown, and neither is `empty_body`, where a body arrived and was
  empty. The `timeout` pattern is widened by one character class, `timed[ _]out`, so that Chromium's
  own `net::ERR_CONNECTION_TIMED_OUT` is a timeout and not an unspecified error. Only codes observed
  on this project's own frames are mapped, for the reason the vendor lists are kept that way, and
  `net::ERR_NAME_NOT_RESOLVED` is deliberately not read as `no_dns`: that name is a claim about the
  organization, and the only evidence this package accepts for it is the resolver answering outright
  that the host does not exist.

  A store written before this build files its transport failures under `unspecified_error`, so the
  two new names are 0 in an older store and that store's `unspecified_error` is larger by however
  many it held. Group the three when comparing an old run with a new one. `READINGS` did not move,
  since a note is not a reading. `FREEZE` moves on the two names, the two patterns, the widened
  `timeout` pattern and the three constants behind the note.

- **A second reading, at the end of a batch, of the batch's own transport failures.** Three
  families in `FAILURE_KINDS` say nothing about the site. Of the 144 addresses the gold-frame run
  recorded `unreachable`, **10 say `Browser.new_context: Connection closed`**, which is the shared
  browser dying under a site that was then written down as unreadable, and **9 of those 10 answered
  a probe** the same day. **11 more end in a timeout, and 10 of those answered a probe within 30
  seconds** against the 240-second bound the run had been given, so what ran out on those was the
  machine at concurrency 8 and not the site. Twenty-one addresses of 144 were recorded against
  organizations on the strength of a failure of the crawl.

  `audit_many_async` now ends each batch by reading those rows once more, on a browser and a driver
  launched for them alone, at `RETRY_PASS_CONCURRENCY`, which is two. A closed connection and a
  protocol error are read back under the run's own per-site clock, since neither of them spent it.
  A row that TIMED OUT is read back on `RETRY_PASS_CLOCK_FACTOR` times that clock, capped at
  `RETRY_PASS_CLOCK_CAP`, which is 600 seconds. `RETRY_PASS_KINDS` is the three, `connection_closed`, `protocol_error`
  and `timeout`. The second reading replaces the first only where it read at least one page, and
  either way the row ends `(retried on a fresh browser)`, so a store shows which rows were offered
  the pass and what came of each. One pass and no more: a row that failed the same way on two
  browsers is a row about the site. Only the rows in the pass are settled late, so `store` and
  `on_result` still receive one row per address and receive it once, which is the property that
  keeps a count of rows a count of addresses.

  Everything else is left where it fell, and the absences are the argument. `bot_wall`, the three
  HTTP families and `empty_body` are the site answering, and answering the same way twice;
  `robots_disallow` is a host's instruction, and a second read would be a second fetch it asked not
  to have; `no_dns`, `parked_domain` and `directory_profile` are settled facts about the address.
  `no_page_any_driver` is left out for a different reason: that name is written only after
  `AUDIT_MAX_ATTEMPTS` whole drivers have read the row and returned nothing, the run's own rule is
  that three drivers reading nothing are a broken machine rather than a frame of dead websites, and
  a pass over those rows would open a fourth driver past the point the run stops at.

  `--no-retry-pass` turns it off, which is how a study measures what it recovers, and `--strict`
  reads the run as the pass left it, so the same list can exit 9 without the pass and 0 with it. A
  machine that cannot start the pass's browser ends the pass and leaves the batch's rows standing,
  since there is nothing to say about a site that was never opened. `READINGS` did not move: the
  pass changes which sites are read, never how a page is judged. `FREEZE` moves by the addition of
  `RETRY_PASS_KINDS`, `RETRY_PASS_CONCURRENCY` and `RETRY_PASS_NOTE`.

  **On the one frame it has been measured on it recovered nothing, and that is stated here rather
  than left to be discovered.** Re-reading those 144 addresses on 2026-09-18 at concurrency 2, the
  pass was offered 12 rows and **none of the 12 read a page on the second browser**. All 12 were
  timeouts, and the driver died under no site in that run, so `connection_closed` and
  `protocol_error` were 0 and the family the pass was built for did not arise. Concurrency 2 is the
  setting at which the shared driver survives, which is exactly why those 21 rows of the earlier
  run were produced at concurrency 8. LIMITATIONS 4 states the same figure.

  **That zero was the clock, and it is why a timed-out row now gets a longer one.** All 12 rows the
  pass was offered had timed out, and it read them back under the bound that had just run out on
  them, which asks a question already answered. The same 12 addresses were read again the same day
  with everything else held identical and `--timeout` at 480 instead of 240: **11 of the 12 read a
  page, at a median of 15 pages**, and 6 of the 11 came back with a language the first run could not
  have seen. The one that did not read is the one whose home page refuses inside 30 seconds, where
  the clock was never the obstacle. The organization-census runbook measures the same effect at
  scale, a 240-second bound cutting 51% of crawls short against 10% at 480. So `RETRY_PASS_SLOW_KINDS`
  names `timeout` as the kind that is read back on `RETRY_PASS_CLOCK_FACTOR` times the run's clock,
  bounded by `RETRY_PASS_CLOCK_CAP` at 600 seconds, and `connection_closed` and `protocol_error`
  keep the run's own bound because neither of them spent it. `FREEZE` moves by the addition of
  those three constants and by nothing else; `RETRY_PASS_KINDS`, `RETRY_PASS_CONCURRENCY` and
  `RETRY_PASS_NOTE` are not in the listing diff. `READINGS` did not move, since no rule reads a
  clock.

- **`no_dns`, a failure kind for a website that is not there to read.** `unreachable` answers one
  question, whether the site was read, and a study reading the answer needs two. Measured over an
  organization census of 32,008 recorded websites, of everything that could not be read, **27% had
  no DNS record at all**. That is a fact about the organization - it had a website and the domain
  lapsed, which for a small nonprofit is a measure of capacity - and no better crawler recovers it.
  The other 73% is a limit of the instrument, and it is the number that belongs beside a recall
  figure. Pooling them flatters the instrument's reach and costs the audit its more interesting
  finding.

  **No verdict moves and no reading moves.** This is not a sixth class. `class_for` derives every
  class from authorship and sufficiency and says in its own docstring that `unreachable` sits
  outside that derivation, because a site that was not read has neither axis to record; a verdict
  for a dead domain would put a third kind of thing into a two-axis scheme. `FAILURE_KINDS` is
  where the package already answers *why* a site was not read, so `no_dns` joins `timeout`,
  `bot_wall` and `http_404` there, and a caller wanting the split writes

  ```python
  r.verdict == 'unreachable' and failure_kind(r) == 'no_dns'
  ```

  `READINGS` did not move. **The 93.2% agreement and κ = 0.896 figures are undisturbed by this
  change.**

  **Two guards, each written after reading what the first attempt produced.** The resolver is asked
  only where nothing already proves the host answered: the first version wrote a fixture reading of
  `HTTP 404 on the home page ... the domain does not resolve`, which cannot both be true, since a
  status code requires a connection. `_REACHED_RX` now stops the question wherever an HTTP status,
  a bot wall or a robots.txt response is already in the note. And the lookup reuses `_resolves`,
  which the package already had for the locale subdomains it invents: it already answers True,
  False, and **None where the resolver did not say either way**, and assembles its
  `_DNS_NO_SUCH_HOST` codes through `getattr` because they differ by platform. Only an outright
  `False` writes the note; a timeout writes nothing, because not knowing a domain is gone is not
  knowing it.

- **`parked_domain`, a failure kind, and `PARKED_HOST`, where the address landed.** Rule 2 has
  written `a parked or expired domain, not the organization's own website` onto a result since the
  release, and `failure_kind` had no name for that note, so every registrar page fell into `other`
  and the family a study most wants to set aside was the one it could not count. The name sits
  beside `directory_profile`, because the two say the same thing about the frame: the address on
  file was never a website the organization runs. The vocabulary goes from thirteen names to
  fourteen.

  The second half is a test the three parking patterns cannot make, because they read the TEXT of a
  page. A domain that has gone to a registrar's marketplace forwards to that marketplace's host, and
  what arrives is the marketplace's own page in whatever wording that company is using this month.
  Over the census render store's 1,162 recorded landings, `hugedomains` accounts for 11,
  `expiredwixdomain` for 6 and `expireddomains` for 2. `PARKED_HOST` holds nine sale and parking
  hosts, matched on the bare host and its `www.` form and never on a suffix, and a home read that
  lands on one is rule 2 and `unreachable` with the same note, whatever the page says.

  No site builder is in the set and none will be. `sites.google.com`, `wixsite.com` and their kind
  are addresses real organizations run their websites on, rule 1 already says a builder address is
  included, and a builder host in this list would report a live organization as unreachable.
  GoDaddy is left to the text test, where `godaddy.com/domainsearch` already reaches the case the
  page shows, because its parked pages are served under names this project cannot state precisely
  and a guess in a frozen set is worse than an absence.

  `FREEZE` is re-recorded, by addition and by nothing else: `FAILURE_KINDS` gains a name,
  `_FAILURE_PATTERNS` gains a pattern, `RULES` names the two new objects under rule 2, and
  `PARKED_HOST` is new. No existing value changed and `READINGS` did not move.

- **A right-hand word boundary on eleven wall alternatives.** The boundary gate in
  `tests/test_pattern_gates.py` has held this family since the release and said in its own docstring
  what it did not cover: a boundary in front of an alternative says nothing about the character
  behind it, so `security check` matched inside `security checklist` under both forms and the gate
  was silent. `found` is the word this was worth doing for, because `founded`, `founder` and
  `foundation` are three of the commonest words on a nonprofit home page and three ungated
  alternatives end on it, which means they decide on their own at any page length.

  The eleven, by pattern. `WALL_UNGATED_RX`: `just a moment` inside `momentary`, `security check`
  inside `security checklist`, `not authorized` inside `not authorized_users`, and `domain not
  found`, `site not found` and `website not found` inside `founded`. `WALL_GATED_RX`:
  `we are (doing some|performing) (work|maintenance|site-wide updates)` inside `we are doing some
  workshops`, and `this site is currently private` inside `currently privately`.
  `WALL_NOTFOUND_RX`: the `requested url/resource/page ... not found` alternative, the `404`
  alternative and `page (not found|cannot be found)`, all inside `founded` and `errors`.

  A boundary went on where the longer word is a different statement, and not where the only longer
  word is a plural or an inflection of the same one. `ddos protections`, `bad gateways` and
  `server errors` say what the shipped alternative says, so a boundary there would lose a real
  catch. Three alternatives that also end on `found` or `exist` were left alone because no sentence
  a person would write extends them: `dns address could not be found`, `deployment cannot be found`
  and `website you requested does not exist`. The rule applied was that a boundary goes on where a
  committed trap sentence can show the reach, and those three have none.

  The census render store is not on this machine, so the corpus count KNOWN_ISSUES asked for could
  not be taken and no figure is given for how many real pages this moves. What is held instead is
  the ward gate over the synthetic corpus, which did not move, and one trap sentence per changed
  alternative in `tests/fixtures/pattern_boundary.json`, checked by a new gate that requires a
  committed string for every right boundary, since a right boundary can only take matches away.
  `FREEZE` is re-recorded on four constants, the three patterns and the `WALL_RX` built from two of
  them. `READINGS` did not move.

- **A challenge that clears mid-poll is a page, and a borrowed browser's page is brought to the
  front.** `_read` polls `inner_text('body')` four times while a Cloudflare interstitial clears
  itself, and a challenge that clears navigates the page, which destroys the execution context the
  poll is holding. Playwright raises `Execution context was destroyed, most likely because of a
  navigation`, that error left `_read`, and the read failed on the good news: the site had just
  become readable. Only that message is caught, and every other error out of `inner_text` still
  leaves the read, because swallowing one would turn a read that did not happen into a reading of an
  empty page.

  The second line is for a borrowed browser, which is a person's own Chrome attached over the
  DevTools protocol by `retry`. A page in a tab nobody is looking at is throttled by that browser,
  so a challenge that has to run JavaScript may never finish; the page is brought to the front
  before the poll, inside a try and except, since bringing a page forward is a thing a browser may
  refuse and no read is worth losing to it. `_read` takes a `borrowed` flag that `_audit_async`
  passes from `borrowed_browser` at all three of its call sites, and it defaults to false, so
  nothing changes for the instrument's own headless browser, which has no foreground.

  Measured in a government session: of five walled sites that had refused a 420-second wait, four
  came back with a page under these two lines in a borrowed browser.

- **`--strict`, an exit code for a run that finished degraded.** A browser driver that dies part
  way through a batch writes honest rows: every site after it answered nothing, too fast, on every
  driver it was offered, and each row says so in its note. The process then exits 0, and a census of
  a dead machine is indistinguishable from a census of dead websites to anything reading the exit
  code. `capture_acceptance` already answers whether a finished run is deep enough to be compared
  with another and already warns when it is not, and a warning on stderr is what a scheduled job's
  log buries; the orgs pilot missed exactly that warning.

  With the flag the run is performed as it is performed without it, every row is written, and the
  process then exits 9 when `capture_acceptance` refuses the run or when any row carries
  `DEAD_DRIVER_NOTE`, with the reason and the counts behind it on stderr. Without the flag nothing
  moves, because a run full of sites that genuinely refuse a crawler is a result and the exit code
  has never claimed otherwise. Codes 3 and 8 outrank 9, since a run that stopped where it stood is
  a fraction of a list rather than a degraded reading of the whole of one; 9 outranks 6, since a
  rejected input says the output does not cover the list and a degraded run says the rows in the
  output cannot be compared with another run's.

  `_run` now hands back one small record per result, `read_quality` and `note`, instead of the
  Result, because a run over ten thousand sites would otherwise hold every evidence list in memory
  to answer two questions at the end.

- **`tool_build` and `judged_build`, the bytes that took a reading and the bytes that judged it.**
  A version label names a release, and on a working tree it names the last release the literal was
  set to. Over one organization census that produced two instruments under one name: **334 records
  carry a reading taken by one `core.py` while the package label beside them says 0.1.0 for
  another**, and nothing on the row said so. The discrepancy was found months later by hashing the
  two files by hand, and it could as easily not have been found at all. `build_id()` is the first
  twelve hex digits of the sha256 of `core.py` as installed, and it is exported, so the value a row
  carries is the value a document can quote.

  The file and not the package, because `core.py` holds the rules, the constants and the crawl,
  while `report.py` renders a finished reading and decides nothing; a build identity that moves for
  reasons unrelated to the reading is one a consumer learns to ignore. Twelve digits because the
  field is read by people comparing two rows. The constant freeze in the test suite stays the finer
  instrument: it says which constant moved, where this says only that something did.

  Both fields are set wherever the two versions were already set, so a live audit stamps them equal
  and `rejudge` carries `tool_build` from the capture and stamps `judged_build` with the build doing
  the judging, exactly as it already did with the versions. `''` is the answer on a record written
  before the fields existed, so an empty `tool_build` beside a filled `judged_build` is a 0.1.0
  store judged by this code. `FREEZE` did not move: the value is cached on the function rather than
  in a module constant, since a constant holding a cache would have re-recorded the gate. `READINGS`
  did not move either, and a test now states why it must not, since the digest would otherwise be
  re-recorded by an edit to a comment in `core.py`.

- **`SETTLE_BY_GROWTH`, off by default: end the settle wait when the document stops being written.**
  `_read` waited a fixed 2,200 ms after navigation and 900 ms after the scroll, on every page of
  every site. Timed over forty sites, the pair costs 4,695 ms a page against 1,944 ms for a wait
  that ends when the body has not grown across two polls - and through `audit` itself, 17.7 seconds
  a site against 13.8.

  `networkidle` was tried first and is the wrong signal: it fires while pages are still writing, and
  every difference it produced was a **loss**, one site returning 2,339 characters where the fixed
  wait had read 3,800. A shorter wait may never read less, because less non-English text is less
  sufficiency and sufficiency decides the class. A wait that ends when growth ends cannot stop
  before the content does.

  **It ships off.** Compared through `audit` on 48 sites, the verdict and the languages were
  identical on all 48 and two sites moved a subsidiary field. Running the SAME arm twice moved
  three, including one `authorship`, so the change is quieter than the instrument's own
  run-to-run variation and n = 48 cannot separate them. The flag exists so the comparison can be
  run without two checkouts.

- **The test suite runs against an installed package.** A copy of `tests/` beside an installed
  wheel is how this package is checked against what a user actually gets, and the suite could not be
  run that way: the subprocess tests ran `python -m langaccess.cli` with the working directory set
  to `src`, so the working directory was what decided which copy of the package answered, and with
  no `src` beside them they failed on `No module named langaccess`. The tests that read `README.md`
  and the web front end failed too, on files that are not in a wheel and were never meant to be.

  `tests/conftest.py` gains `cli_argv`, `cli_env` and `repo_file`. The subprocess form is
  `python -m langaccess` with `PYTHONPATH` naming the tree when the tree is there and naming nothing
  when it is not, so the same test reads the working copy in a checkout and the installed package in
  a detached run, and no test depends on where it was started from. A test whose subject is the
  repository rather than the package skips with the path it wanted: the README codebook table, and
  the web front end, which is not part of the wheel. The demo capture is resolved through
  `langaccess.__file__` instead of through `src/`, because it ships inside the wheel and the two
  paths are the same file only in a checkout.

  Measured by running the suite three ways on this machine, on the tree this change was made on,
  where the suite is smaller than it is after the merges below. From the worktree root and from
  `tests/` it reads 1,503 passed, 5 skipped, 15 deselected. From a copy of `tests/` with no
  repository beside it and the package on `PYTHONPATH`, it reads 1,410 passed, 7 skipped, 15
  deselected and nothing failed; the difference is the two modules that skip whole, the README
  table and the web front end.

- **`dead_control_no_vendor`, a seventh hand-coding kind, for the absence claim nobody was sent to
  check.** Rule 16 reads `a control of a NAMED WIDGET that was operated and changed nothing`, so on
  a site where no vendor pattern matched, a control that was clicked and did nothing cannot produce
  `machine_translate_error` and the reading falls to `english_only`. That is the one class which
  asserts an absence, and it was asserting it over the clearest reason to doubt it: the observation
  sat on the record twice, as evidence with rule 16 on it and as the note sentence, decided nothing,
  and reached nobody.

  Measured over the 2,000-site gold frame on 2026-09-18, and the boundary is most of the decision.
  88 readings record a dead control. 33 are `machine_translate_error` and already queued as
  `dead_control`. **41 are left alone**, 40 `true_multilingual` and 1 `machine_translate`, because
  those verdicts rest on something that was FOUND and a control that did nothing settles nothing
  about them, which is the same boundary `needs_human` already applies to a thin search. **14 are
  the new kind**, every one of them `english_only` with no vendor named; 4 of the 14 were queued
  before as `thin_absence` and keep both sentences, and **10 were queued by nothing**. The queue
  over that frame goes from 261 rows to 271. An `english_only` where a vendor IS named and a
  control died is excluded on purpose: rule 15 put it there on the server's own answer to an
  advertised locale route, which is the stronger observation and a settled reading.

  A seventh kind and not a widening of `dead_control`, because that kind's sentence opens `a
  translation widget is on this site` and these are the rows where none was found. `unsettled_kind`
  now reads the note and `machine_translation` as well, off the same `CONTROL_DEAD_NOTE` constant
  the three verdict derivations read rule 16 off, so the queue and the class cannot drift apart.
  **No verdict moves**: this predicate writes none, and over the gold frame every one of the 1,993
  classes is unchanged. Neither freeze gate moves either, and `FREEZE` is not re-recorded, because
  the constants are in `review.py` and the fingerprint is derived from `core.py`; the listing was
  dumped on this revision and on the one before it and is identical. The other two frames the tests
  cover hold no dead-control reading at all, so neither queue changes: the 42 readings of
